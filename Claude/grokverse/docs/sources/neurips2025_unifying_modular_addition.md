## 0. Answer in one paragraph

The clearly matching NeurIPS 2025 paper is **McCracken, Moisescu-Pareja, Létourneau, Precup, Love — "Uncovering a Universal Abstract Algorithm for Modular Addition in Neural Networks", arXiv:2505.18266, NeurIPS 2025 main-conference poster** (neurips.cc poster 115005; OpenReview zuHs6RHQwT). It covers both MLPs (1–4 layers) and transformers, and its MLPs are exactly concatenation MLPs: the inputs are "either one-hot encoded or embedded via a learned matrix with n rows and 128-dimensional vectors, resulting in concatenated input pairs (E_a, E_b)" (Section 3). Its unifying claim is that all of these implement an "approximate Chinese Remainder Theorem" (aCRT) built from single-frequency ("simple") sinusoidal neurons, with a phase transition from ⌊n/2⌋ learned frequencies (one-hot, one hidden layer) to O(log n) frequencies (trainable embedding **or** ≥2 hidden layers). It does **not** discuss harmonics or square waves (the fetched text has none; it does discuss "secondary spikes in the DFT" that fade with width, Fig. 5), and it contains **no explicit metric-validity discussion** (its evidence is R² of single-sinusoid fits and DFT peak counting). Two companion works by the same group are the closest alternatives: a NeurIPS 2025 **WiML affinity-event** poster (transformers only) and a **Dec 2025 arXiv preprint (2512.25060, no venue)** that adds an explicit **MLP-Concat** arm and finds that it is the one architecture whose first-layer representation is a **torus rather than a disc** — i.e. the a- and b-paths stay independent before the nonlinearity, which is the same structural fact GROKVERSE's `analysis/mlp_mechanism.py` docstring derives. The square-wave framing that GROKVERSE's spec attributes to arXiv:2603.23784 **is confirmed** for the two-hot (one-hot-concatenated) ReLU MLP by that paper's abstract and model section, but that paper is a 9-page March 2026 preprint, not NeurIPS 2025.

(2) The training hyperparameters are **not** missing from the source: Appendix E gives them (Adam; 90 %/10 % split; 1024 neurons; L2 = 1e-5; lr = 0.00075 for the Fig. 4 MLPs; see §1.6). ### 1.2 Abstract (verbatim)

> "We propose a testable universality hypothesis, asserting that seemingly disparate neural network solutions observed in the simple task of modular addition are unified under a common abstract algorithm. While prior work interpreted variations in neuron-level representations as evidence for distinct algorithms, we demonstrate—through multi-level analyses spanning neurons, neuron clusters, and entire networks—that multilayer perceptrons and transformers universally implement the abstract algorithm we call the approximate Chinese Remainder Theorem. Crucially, we introduce approximate cosets and show that neurons activate exclusively on them. Furthermore, our theory works for deep neural networks (DNNs). It predicts that universally learned solutions in DNNs with trainable embeddings or more than one hidden layer require only 𝒪(log n) features, a result we empirically confirm. This work thus provides the first theory-backed interpretation of multilayer networks solving modular addition. It advances generalizable interpretability and opens a testable universality hypothesis for group multiplication beyond modular addition."

## 4. Consequences for GROKVERSE

GROKVERSE's two MLPs (from the repo): `models/mlp.py` `TwoLayerMLP` — shared `W_E ∈ ℝ^{113×128}`, `x = cat(W_E[a], W_E[b]) ∈ ℝ^{256}`, one ReLU layer of `d_mlp = 512`, linear readout to 113 classes; `models/mlp_twohot.py` `TwoHotMLP` — `x = cat(onehot(a), onehot(b)) ∈ ℝ^{226}`, same hidden layer, no embedding. Canonical regime: p = 113, weight_decay = 1.0, train_frac = 0.3 (or 0.5 with Grokfast), Nanda-recipe transformer as the comparison arm; headline observation is top-8 Fourier concentration 0.73 (transformer) vs 0.44 (MLP) (`docs/RESEARCH_SPEC.md` §2/§4).

**What the identified work already establishes about concatenation MLPs**

1. *The architecture is not exotic — it is the paper's own MLP.* McCracken et al. (NeurIPS 2025) train exactly "concatenated input pairs (𝐄ₐ,𝐄ᵦ)" (one-hot or 128-d learned embeddings, 1–4 layers) and claim they implement the aCRT (Sec. 3, Alg. 4.6). GROKVERSE's `TwoLayerMLP` corresponds to their "trainable embedding, 1 hidden layer" cell, `TwoHotMLP` to their "one-hot, 1 hidden layer" cell. The related-work section can therefore say the concatenation MLP is a standard object in this literature, not a GROKVERSE idiosyncrasy.
2. *Frequency count is predicted to differ sharply between GROKVERSE's two MLPs.* "adding either depth, or a trainable embedding matrix for inputs, causes the network to transition from learning ⌊n/2⌋ types of neurons to much fewer types" (Sec. 4.4). So `TwoLayerMLP` (trainable embedding) is expected to learn a "handful" (their phrase: "3-7") of frequencies, while `TwoHotMLP` is expected to learn ≈⌊113/2⌋ = 56 frequencies (consistent with Morwani et al. Thm. 7: "For every frequency ζ ... at least one neuron in the network uses this frequency"). Practical consequence: **any top-k Fourier-concentration number for `TwoHotMLP` is expected to be low by construction**, and this is not evidence of "less structure" — a point that strengthens H3's motivation but must be argued from these sources rather than from GROKVERSE's own data.
3. *The additivity-before-ReLU fact is published, as a torus.* GROKVERSE's `mlp_mechanism.py` argues "the MLP is exactly additive in (a, b) up to the ReLU". Moisescu-Pareja et al. (2512.25060, Thm. 4.1 / Eq. 6) formalise the same fact: MLP-Concat preactivations factor as "(cos θ_a, sin θ_a, cos θ_b, sin θ_b)" (rank 4, a torus), whereas MLP-Add/Clock/Pizza factor as "(cos θ_a + cos θ_b, sin θ_a + sin θ_b)" (rank 2, a disc); "For MLP-Concat, the first four principal components each explain about 25% of variance, producing a toroidal structure." Two direct consequences: (i) GROKVERSE's `pca_ring` figures for the MLP — which project `W_E` or activations onto 2 PCs — are the wrong picture for a concat MLP; the per-frequency cluster should show ~25 % explained variance on each of four PCs, not a 2-D ring; (ii) the "MLP is a more distributed solution" sentence in the current repo is contradicted at the representation level: the same group says "all use the same torus to logits map. The transformers and MLP-Add architecture simply learn lower rank manifolds of the same class of torus manifolds".
4. *MLP-Concat is the outlier architecture in that group's own statistics.* "MLP-Concat is strongly separated from all others" in the Phase Alignment Distribution and its "clusters activate almost equivariantly" rather than on the a = b diagonal (Sec. 4.2, 6.1). GROKVERSE's transformer-vs-MLP contrast is therefore the *largest* contrast available in this family, which is good for the headline but means transformer-derived analysis code (diagonal-concentrated activations, 2-D rings) transfers least well to precisely this model.
5. *The square-wave framing (H2) is now verified for the two-hot ReLU MLP.* Swaroop (2603.23784) states for a 194-d two-hot input, 256-ReLU, p = 97 MLP that "input weights are near-binary square waves rather than cosines" and that "the ϕ_out = ϕ_a + ϕ_b relation is preserved". H1's phase-sum prediction (`mlp_mechanism.py`) and H2's square-wave framing both have a primary source; the spec's `[UNVERIFIED]` tag on line 188 can be lifted **for the two-hot model only**. Gromov (2301.02679) gives the same phase constraint φ⁽¹⁾+φ⁽²⁾ = φ⁽³⁾ analytically for the quadratic-activation concat MLP.

**What remains open (not settled by any fetched source)**

1. *Square waves / odd harmonics with a trainable shared embedding.* McCracken et al.: harmonics/square waves "Not present in page text"; only "secondary spikes in the DFT" that fade with width (Fig. 5). Moisescu-Pareja et al.: "No passages discuss square waves". Swaroop: one-hot input only. Whether GROKVERSE's `TwoLayerMLP` (embedding + concat) produces square-wave-like *effective* curves `u_a = W_E · W_in[:128, i]` is unaddressed — this is the gap H2/H3 target.
2. *Harmonic content of the "secondary spikes".* No source says whether the secondary DFT peaks sit at odd multiples of the primary frequency, nor gives amplitude ratios. Swaroop's paper compares a raw square wave against a cosine by nearest-point distance and does not state an odd-harmonic / 1/j decomposition — the spec's "amplitudes decay approximately as 1/j" (line 189) has **no primary-source support** and should be presented as GROKVERSE's own prediction from the Fourier series of a square wave, not as a literature claim.
3. *Validity of top-k Fourier concentration as a cross-architecture metric.* McCracken et al. use only R² of simple-neuron fits and DFT peak counts; Moisescu-Pareja et al. show two of Zhong et al.'s metrics "[do not distinguish] between Attention 1.0 and 0.0 models" (App. E.2) and replace them with PAD / persistent homology, but never examine Fourier-concentration metrics. H3 (harmonic-family concentration with a cardinality-matched top-m control) is not pre-empted.
4. *Width dependence.* McCracken et al. tie secondary spikes to small width ("These occur less often as the width of the layers is increased (Fig 5)"); Moisescu-Pareja et al. use 1024 neurons; GROKVERSE uses 512. A width sweep (and the existing `d_mlp = 572` parameter-matched control) is the cheapest check that 0.44 is not simply a finite-width secondary-spike effect.
5. *Regime mismatch.* McCracken et al.: "The dataset includes all n² input pairs" (train/test split not recovered), moduli 59–66, L2 strength not recovered; Moisescu-Pareja et al.: n = 59, 90 %/10 % split, L2 = 1e-4 for MLPs, Adam. GROKVERSE: p = 113, train_frac 0.3/0.5, weight_decay 1.0 (AdamW), Grokfast in one arm. None of the sources covers the strong-weight-decay grokking regime at these train fractions, so the *timing* comparison (transformer generalises ~1.3× earlier) has no counterpart in this literature.
6. *Why the ⌊n/2⌋ → O(log n) transition happens* is open by the authors' own statement ("we don't know why it occurs", Sec. 5.1), as is the number of neurons needed per frequency — both are places where a two-model, multi-seed, causal-ablation study like GROKVERSE's could add evidence.
7. *Whether McCracken et al.'s embedding matrix is shared across the a/b slots* — [NOT FOUND IN SOURCE]; if it is not shared, GROKVERSE's shared-`W_E` design is a (minor) architectural difference to flag in the related-work paragraph. Moisescu-Pareja et al. do state a shared matrix.
